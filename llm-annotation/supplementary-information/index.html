Supplementary Information

The following section contains the Supplementary Information of the manuscript: Figures S1 to S4 and Tables S1 to S14. All figures and tables are produced by the master script documented in the section Reproduction Materials.

Figures

Fig. S1

Expert ratings of the clarity and completeness of the prompts for the 17 variables (N = 17 experts). Circles represent variables, numbered and colored by level of analysis as in Fig. 1A; error bars show 95% confidence intervals of the mean. The key lists the variables.

Fig. S2

LLM–human agreement per variable for majority-vote LLM coding, Gemini, GPT, and Qwen. (A) Krippendorff's alpha; the dashed line indicates the conventional threshold for acceptable reliability (α = 0.60). (B) Percentage agreement; the dashed line indicates 80% agreement. Variables are numbered and colored by level of analysis as in Fig. 1.

Fig. S3

Need for information, need for interpretation, and LLM–human agreement, measured as percentage agreement (0–100), for majority-vote LLM coding, Gemini, GPT, and Qwen. (A) Bivariate regressions on need for information. (B) Bivariate regressions on need for interpretation. In A and B, points represent variables, solid lines indicate fitted linear regression lines, and shaded bands denote 95% confidence intervals; panel headers report standardized coefficients (β) and p values. (C) Standardized coefficients (β) from models including both dimensions jointly; horizontal bars denote 95% confidence intervals. n = 17 variables per model. Corresponding results for Krippendorff's alpha are shown in Fig. 2.

Fig. S4

Need for information and need for interpretation when controlling for the clarity and completeness of the prompts. Standardized coefficients (β) with 95% confidence intervals from models including all four expert ratings, for (A) Krippendorff's alpha and (B) percentage agreement, for majority-vote LLM coding, Gemini, GPT, and Qwen. n = 17 variables per model. Full results in Tables S10 and S13.
