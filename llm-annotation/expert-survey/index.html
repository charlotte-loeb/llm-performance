Expert Survey

The following section presents information on the expert survey conducted to assess the complexity of the text annotation.

Sampling frame

56 experts in manual content analysis:

  • Authors listed in the Database of Variables for Content Analysis (DOCA), sections
    • News/Journalism
    • Fiction/Entertainment
    • User-Generated Media Content
  • Experts known to the authors who have published content analysis studies

Data collection

Mode Online survey (LimeSurvey)
Contact E-mail (51 experts) and LinkedIn (5 experts), two reminders
Field period 16 April – 7 May 2026
Incentive Prize draw of 15 gift cards worth €10 each; e-mail addresses for the draw were stored separately from the survey data and deleted afterwards
Rated material The prompts for the 17 variables on four levels (text, actor, justification, actor reference). Each prompt was preceded by a note on the material available to coders (e.g. full article text, validated list of actors).

Questionnaire

The questionnaire was in English. Its introduction asked the experts to assess three aspects of each coding task: the required external or background knowledge, the scope for interpretation, and the operationality of the coding instructions (clarity of the instructions and completeness of the decision rules). Experts were asked to base their ratings on a competent trained coder who has access only to the media text and the coding instructions shown in the survey. The survey took about 20–30 minutes.

Each of the 17 variables was presented on its own page with the variable name, the coding instruction and the codes, reproduced from an existing codebook. A note to experts described the material available to coders:

Level Variables Material available to coders
Text Section, Form of presentation, Tone (outrage intensity), Opposing positions Full article text; for Section and Tone also the metadata (URL, publication date, title, subtitle, author and, where available, the section label of the outlet); for Opposing positions also a validated list of the positions identified in the article
Actor Actor role, Actor party, Actor origin, Actor gender, Actor religion Article text and a validated list of the actors identified in the article; coded separately for each actor. For Actor party, coders also have a list of all parties in the political system (not shown in the survey)
Justification Justification in-group, out-group, common good, religious beliefs Article text and a validated list of the justifications identified in the article; coded separately for each justification
Actor reference Actor reference responsiveness (genuine/constructed), valence, recognition, outrage Article text and a validated list of the actor-to-actor references identified in the article; coded separately for each reference

After each variable, experts answered four questions on five-point scales:

How clearly are the coding instructions formulated? Clarity clear
How complete are the decision rules provided in the coding instructions? Completeness complete

Expert agreement

The table shows how closely the experts agreed in their ratings of the 17 prompts, separately for each rating dimension. Krippendorff's alpha (interval metric) and the intraclass correlation of a single expert's rating, ICC(2,1), describe the agreement between individual experts. The ICC(2,k) describes the reliability of the mean rating across all 17 experts, which is the value used in the analyses. Agreement between individual experts is low to moderate, but the mean ratings are reliable (ICC(2,k) = .73 to .95).
